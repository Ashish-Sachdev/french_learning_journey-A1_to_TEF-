# 1. Basic Pronunciation Rules

## Final consonants

French final consonants are **often silent**, but there are many exceptions.

### 🧠 CRFL memory rule

A useful beginner memory aid is **C, R, F, L**: these final consonants are **often** pronounced.

```text
C  R  F  L
↓  ↓  ↓  ↓
Careful! These are often heard at the end.
```

> ⚠️ **Tutor note:** CRFL is only a shortcut, not a guaranteed rule. Learn pronunciation word by word as your vocabulary grows.

## Final **e**

A final **e** is usually **silent**, not normally an English “uh.”

- **table** → **TAHBL**
- **petite** → **puh-TEET**
- **robe** → **ROHB**
- **pomme** → **POM**

> ⚠️ **Tutor correction:** In careful or poetic speech, a final *e* may sometimes be heard, but for normal modern spoken French it is usually silent.

# 2. French Accents

## A. L'accent aigu — **é**

### Rule

**é** normally represents the French **/e/** sound. For an English speaker, **“ay”** is a useful approximation, but the French sound is **steadier and does not glide** like the vowel in English *day*.

### 🧠 Mouth position

```text
é → mouth slightly more closed
è → mouth slightly more open
```

### Examples

| French | English | Gender / Type | Pronunciation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **été** | summer | masculine — **un été** | **ay-TAY** | Both **é** sounds are clear. |
| **médecin** | doctor | person noun | **med-SAN** | **un médecin / une médecin** depending on the person. |
| **élève** | pupil / student | masculine or feminine | **ay-LEV** | **un élève / une élève**. |
| **café** | coffee / café | masculine — **un café** | **ka-FAY** | Final **é** is pronounced. |
| **écouter** | to listen | verb | **ay-koo-TAY** | Infinitive. |
| **marché** | market | masculine — **un marché** | **mar-SHAY** | Do not confuse with **marcher** = to walk. |
| **fée** | fairy | feminine — **une fée** | **FAY** | Final **e** after **é** is silent. |
| **clé** | key | feminine — **une clé** | **CLAY** | Also spelled **clef**. |
| **dé** | die / dice cube | masculine — **un dé** | **DAY** | **de** = from/of, roughly **duh**. |

## B. L'accent grave — **è, à, ù**

### Rule

On **è**, the accent usually gives the more open French **/ɛ/** sound, approximately like **“eh”** in English *bed*.

The accents on **à** and **ù** mainly distinguish words; they do not create the same vowel change as **è**.

### Important word pairs

| Without accent | With accent | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | **à** | **a** = has; **à** = to / at |
| **la** | **là** | **la** = the; **là** = there |
| **ou** | **où** | **ou** = or; **où** = where |

> 🗣️ In these pairs, the accented and unaccented forms are usually pronounced the same.

### Examples

| French | English | Gender / Type | Pronunciation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **très** | very | adverb | **treh** | Final **s** silent. |
| **père** | father | masculine — **un père** | **pehr** | Open **è**. |
| **mère** | mother | feminine — **une mère** | **mehr** | Open **è**. |
| **problème** | problem | masculine — **un problème** | **pro-BLEM** | Final **e** silent. |
| **après** | after | preposition/adverb | **ah-PREH** | Final **s** silent. |
| **près** | near | adverb/preposition | **preh** | Final **s** silent. |
| **dès** | from / starting from | preposition | **deh** | Example: **dès demain**. |
| **accès** | access | masculine — **un accès** | **ak-SEH** | Final **s** silent. |
| **collège** | middle school / college depending on context | masculine — **un collège** | **ko-LEZH** | In France, usually middle school. |
| **succès** | success | masculine — **un succès** | **sook-SEH** | Final **s** silent. |

## D. Le tréma — **ë, ï, ü, ö, ÿ**

### Rule

The **tréma (¨)** often tells you that a vowel must be treated separately from a neighbouring vowel.

```text
égoïste → é-go-ïste → ay-go-EEST
Haïti   → Ha-ï-ti   → ah-ee-TEE
```

### Examples

| French | English | Gender / Type | Pronunciation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Noël** | Christmas | masculine — **Noël** | **no-EL** | **o** and **ë** are heard separately. |
| **maïs** | corn | masculine — **le maïs** | **mah-EESS** | Vowels separated. |
| **naïf / naïve** | naive | adjective | **nah-EEF / nah-EEV** | Masculine / feminine. |
| **Haïti** | Haiti | place name | **ah-ee-TEE** | Usually **à Haïti**. |
| **Israël** | Israel | place name | **ees-rah-EL** | Usually **en Israël**. |
| **égoïste** | selfish | adjective, same form M/F | **ay-go-EEST** | **Il/Elle est égoïste.** |
| **coïncidence** | coincidence | feminine — **une coïncidence** | **ko-ahn-see-DAHNS** | Separate **o + ï**. |
| **ambiguë** | ambiguous | feminine adjective | **ahn-bee-GOO** | Masculine: **ambigu**. |
| **aiguë** | acute / sharp | feminine adjective | **ay-GOO** | Masculine: **aigu**. |
| **héroïne** | heroine | feminine — **une héroïne** | **ay-ro-EEN** | Do not merge vowels around **ï**. |

## E. La cédille — **ç**

### Rule

Normally:

```text
c + a / o / u → hard K sound
ca, co, cu

c + e / i / y → soft S sound
ce, ci, cy

ç + a / o / u → S sound
ça, ço, çu
```

So **ç** is mainly needed before **a, o, u** to force the **S** sound.

### Examples

| French | English | Gender / Type | Pronunciation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **garçon** | boy | masculine — **un garçon** | **gar-SON** | **ç** = S sound. |
| **français** | French | language/adjective | **frahn-SEH** | **le français** = the French language. |
| **façade** | façade/front | feminine — **une façade** | **fa-SAD** | |
| **leçon** | lesson | feminine — **une leçon** | **luh-SON** | |
| **reçu** | received / receipt | participle or noun | **ruh-SY** | **un reçu** = a receipt. |
| **ça** | this / that / it | pronoun | **sah** | Very common in speech. |
| **maçon** | mason | masculine — **un maçon** | **ma-SON** | |
| **glaçon** | ice cube | masculine — **un glaçon** | **gla-SON** | |
| **menaçant** | threatening | adjective | **muh-na-SAHN** | |
| **lançons** | we throw / we launch | verb — **nous lançons** | **lahn-SON** | Infinitive = **lancer**. |

## 4. **on** → nasal vowel

> ⚠️ **Tutor correction:** Do **not** actually pronounce an English **g** in “ong.” The spelling **ong** is only a memory aid. The French sound is nasal: air comes partly through the nose, and the final **n** is normally not pronounced.

English "on" → tongue finishes with N

## 5. **an, en** → nasal vowel

> This is different from **on**. For **an/en**, open your mouth a little more and keep the vowel deeper/backer. Again, do **not** add an English **n** or **g** at the end.

```text
on     → rounder lips, more "ohn"-like nasal vowel
an/en  → mouth more open, deeper "ahn"-like nasal vowel
```

### Practice contrast

**bon** → nasal **on**  
**banc** → nasal **an**

Say them slowly and keep the final consonant silent.

### **Il y a du vent**

**Natural English:** It's windy.  
**Literal English:** There is some wind.

### 🎂 What does **anniversaire** really mean?

Your teacher's point was probably that French **anniversaire** is broader than English **anniversary**.

- **un anniversaire** = the anniversary of an event, something returning each year
- **un anniversaire de naissance / mon anniversaire** = birthday

In everyday French, **mon anniversaire** almost always means **my birthday**.

**C'est mon anniversaire aujourd'hui.** → It's my birthday today.

# 4. Extra Pattern Notes

## English loanwords

Your class note says English words borrowed into French are masculine. **Many common loanwords are masculine**, for example:

- **le parking**
- **le T-shirt**
- **le week-end**
- **le shopping**
- **le camping**

> ⚠️ **Tutor note:** This is a tendency, **not an absolute rule**. Do not assume every borrowed English noun must be masculine.

## Letter **g**

Usually:

```text
g + a / o / u → hard G sound

g + e / i / y → soft ZH sound
```

Examples:
- **gare** → hard G
- **gomme** → hard G
- **guitare** → hard G
- **girafe** → soft ZH
- **général** → soft ZH

# 6. Le passé récent

## Usage

The **passé récent** expresses something that **just happened**.

English often uses:

**have/has just + past participle**

French uses:

**venir (present tense) + de + infinitive**

### Structure

```text
Subject + present tense of venir + de/d' + infinitive
```

### Present tense of **venir**

| Subject | Venir | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| je | **viens** | **vyan** |
| tu | **viens** | **vyan** |
| il / elle / on | **vient** | **vyan** |
| nous | **venons** | **vuh-NON** |
| vous | **venez** | **vuh-NAY** |
| ils / elles | **viennent** | **vyen** |

> Notice: **je viens**, **tu viens**, and **il/elle vient** sound the same even though the spelling changes.

## Examples

1. **I just saw Luc.**  
   **Je viens de voir Luc.**  
   Pronunciation: **zhuh vyan duh vwar Look**

2. **He just arrived.**  
   **Il vient d'arriver.**  
   Pronunciation: **eel vyan da-ree-VAY**

3. **We just prepared the meal.**  
   **Nous venons de préparer le repas.**

4. **He learned the news a moment ago.**  
   **Il vient d'apprendre la nouvelle.**

5. **You just drank your coffee.**  
   **Tu viens de boire ton café.**  
   > ⚠️ Correction: **boire**, not *boir*.

6. **They (f.) just wrote a letter.**  
   **Elles viennent d'écrire une lettre.**

7. **You just did a French exercise.**  
   **Vous venez de faire un exercice de français.**

8. **I just listened to the professor speak.**  
   **Je viens d'écouter le professeur parler.**

### 🧠 Important idea

The action after **de** stays in the **infinitive**:

```text
Je viens de manger.     ✅
Je viens de voir Luc.   ✅
Je viens de finir.      ✅
```

Do not conjugate the second verb.

# 🎯 TEF Speaking Connection

> **Tutor-added notes**

These pronunciation patterns are not tested as isolated theory in the TEF speaking section, but they directly affect **clarity, intelligibility, rhythm, and confidence**.

For CLB 7 speaking, focus especially on:

- pronouncing familiar words as complete sound units instead of letter-by-letter;
- distinguishing nasal vowels such as **on** vs **an/en**;
- avoiding English glides in French vowels such as **é**;
- using connectors and complete sentences;
- learning each noun together with its gender;
- moving beyond the present tense by using structures such as **passé récent**, **passé composé**, **imparfait**, and later the conditional/subjunctive.
