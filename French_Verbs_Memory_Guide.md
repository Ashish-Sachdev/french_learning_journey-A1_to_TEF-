# French present-tense verbs: connections, patterns and practice

*A beginner-friendly guide to all 36 verbs in your list, with English translations.*

## 1. Learn families, not 216 separate boxes

Your table is correct. One classification detail: **être and avoir are usually treated separately as auxiliaries**, while the other verbs here belong to the third group. **Aller belongs to the third group even though it ends in -er.** The families below are memory aids for the present tense, not rules for every tense.

Learn each verb in this order:

1. **Meaning + one real sentence:** say *Je prends le bus* and imagine your commute.
2. **Je, nous, ils:** these forms often reveal the different stems: *je bois → nous buvons → ils boivent*.
3. **Complete the six forms:** then check the exceptions, especially *vous faites* and *vous dites*.
4. **Recall without looking:** translate a short sentence, then check it.

**Pronouns:** je = I; tu = you (one person, informal); il/elle = he/she; on = one/we/people; nous = we; vous = you (formal or plural); ils/elles = they. In everyday conversation, *on* often means *we*, but always takes the il/elle/on verb form: *on prend le bus* = we take the bus.

### Pattern map

| Family | Verbs | Connection to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essential foundations | être, avoir, aller, faire | Identity, possession, movement, action. Learn these as frequent chunks. |
| Ability, desire, obligation | pouvoir, vouloir, devoir | **Je peux / je veux / je dois + infinitive**: I can / want to / have to. |
| Two kinds of knowing | savoir, connaître | **Savoir**: facts or how to do something. **Connaître**: familiarity with someone/something. |
| Coming and holding | venir, tenir | viens/tiens; venons/tenons; viennent/tiennent. |
| Taking in knowledge | prendre, apprendre, comprendre | prends → apprends → comprends; prenons → apprenons → comprenons. |
| Putting | mettre | mets/met in singular; mettons/mettez/mettent in plural. |
| Communication | dire, lire, écrire | Say, read, write. Similar singular endings, different plural stems. |
| Seeing and believing | voir, croire | vois/crois; voyons/croyons; voient/croient. |
| Three drinking stems | boire | boi- / buv- / boiv-. |
| Living and following | vivre, suivre | vis/vivons/vivent; suis/suivons/suivent. |
| A reusable -dre pattern | attendre, entendre, répondre, perdre | -s, -s, no added ending, -ons, -ez, -ent. |
| Receiving | recevoir | reçois / recevons / reçoivent. |
| Short singular stems | dormir, partir, sortir, sentir | dor-/dorm-, par-/part-, sor-/sort-, sen-/sent-. |
| -ir verbs with -er present endings | ouvrir, offrir | ouvre/offre; ouvres/offres; ouvrons/offrons. |
| Running | courir | cour- stays stable throughout the present. |
| Driving | conduire | conduis/conduit; conduisons/conduisent. |

### Sound helps you remember

- **Different spelling often means the same sound:** *prends, prends, prend* sound alike; so do *peux, peux, peut*.
- **Verb endings -ent are silent:** *ils parlent* does not end with a spoken “ent.” However, a changed stem may be audible: *il prend* and *ils prennent* sound different.
- **Final written consonants are often silent:** do not automatically pronounce the s or t in *lis, lit, dois, doit*.
- **Nous -ons has a nasal vowel; vous -ez usually sounds roughly like “ay.”** These are cues, not exact English equivalents.
- **Je becomes j’ before a vowel sound:** *j’ai, j’apprends, j’entends, j’écris, j’ouvre, j’offre*.
- Learn *vous êtes* as roughly “voo zet” and *vous avez* as “voo zah-vay”: the linking z is heard. No capital letters are used here to imply English-style word stress.

## 2. A connection and an example for every verb

**Each form line follows this order:** je → tu → il/elle/on → nous → vous → ils/elles. Add the subject before each form; use j’ when needed.

### 01. être — to be

**Present:** suis · es · est · sommes · êtes · sont

**Memory connection / tip:** Identity: être tells you who you are or how you feel. Learn je suis as one chunk.

**Example:** Je suis étudiant.  
**English:** I am a student.

### 02. avoir — to have

**Present:** ai · as · a · avons · avez · ont

**Memory connection / tip:** Possession, age and several feelings use avoir: j’ai un téléphone, j’ai faim, j’ai 25 ans. Remember j’ai, never je ai.

**Example:** J’ai faim.  
**English:** I am hungry.

### 03. aller — to go

**Present:** vais · vas · va · allons · allez · vont

**Memory connection / tip:** Movement, then plans: aller + infinitive describes what you are going to do.

**Example:** Je vais apprendre le français.  
**English:** I am going to learn French.

### 04. faire — to do / to make

**Present:** fais · fais · fait · faisons · faites · font

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect it to activities: faire du sport, faire la cuisine. Watch vous faites; faisons is pronounced roughly fuh-zon, with a nasal ending.

**Example:** Je fais du sport.  
**English:** I exercise.

### 05. pouvoir — can / to be able to

**Present:** peux · peux · peut · pouvons · pouvez · peuvent

**Memory connection / tip:** Ability: can I do it? Pair it with vouloir and devoir. Pouvoir + infinitive; no de or à between them.

**Example:** Je peux venir demain.  
**English:** I can come tomorrow.

### 06. vouloir — to want

**Present:** veux · veux · veut · voulons · voulez · veulent

**Memory connection / tip:** Desire: do I want to do it? Learn je veux + infinitive. Je voudrais is a useful, more polite form meaning I would like; it is not present tense.

**Example:** Je veux apprendre le français.  
**English:** I want to learn French.

### 07. devoir — must / to have to / to owe

**Present:** dois · dois · doit · devons · devez · doivent

**Memory connection / tip:** Obligation: must I do it? Devoir + infinitive expresses a duty; devoir + money can mean owe.

**Example:** Je dois partir.  
**English:** I have to leave.

### 08. savoir — to know a fact / to know how

**Present:** sais · sais · sait · savons · savez · savent

**Memory connection / tip:** Facts and skills: savoir + infinitive means know how to. Connect it with English savvy as a memory cue.

**Example:** Je sais conduire.  
**English:** I know how to drive.

### 09. connaître — to know / to be familiar with

**Present:** connais · connais · connaît · connaissons · connaissez · connaissent

**Memory connection / tip:** People, places and familiarity: think acquaintance. Contrast je connais cette personne with je sais son nom.

**Example:** Je connais cette ville.  
**English:** I know this city.

### 10. venir — to come

**Present:** viens · viens · vient · venons · venez · viennent

**Memory connection / tip:** Movement toward here. Venir de + infinitive means have just done something.

**Example:** Je viens de finir.  
**English:** I have just finished.

### 11. tenir — to hold / to keep

**Present:** tiens · tiens · tient · tenons · tenez · tiennent

**Memory connection / tip:** Imagine holding something. Swap v for t in venir’s forms: viens → tiens, venons → tenons.

**Example:** Je tiens mon téléphone.  
**English:** I am holding my phone.

### 12. prendre — to take

**Present:** prends · prends · prend · prenons · prenez · prennent

**Memory connection / tip:** The parent of apprendre and comprendre: learn its forms once, then attach a prefix.

**Example:** Je prends le bus.  
**English:** I take the bus.

### 13. apprendre — to learn / to teach

**Present:** apprends · apprends · apprend · apprenons · apprenez · apprennent

**Memory connection / tip:** Think take in knowledge. Add ap- to prendre’s forms. Apprendre à + infinitive means learn to do something.

**Example:** J’apprends à conduire.  
**English:** I am learning to drive.

### 14. comprendre — to understand

**Present:** comprends · comprends · comprend · comprenons · comprenez · comprennent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to comprehend. Add com- to prendre’s forms. Useful rescue phrase: je ne comprends pas.

**Example:** Je comprends la question.  
**English:** I understand the question.

### 15. mettre — to put / to put on

**Present:** mets · mets · met · mettons · mettez · mettent

**Memory connection / tip:** Put an object somewhere or put clothes on. Singular forms have one t; plural forms have two.

**Example:** Je mets ma veste.  
**English:** I am putting on my jacket.

### 16. dire — to say / to tell

**Present:** dis · dis · dit · disons · dites · disent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to dialogue. The trap is vous dites, not disez.

**Example:** Je dis bonjour.  
**English:** I say hello.

### 17. lire — to read

**Present:** lis · lis · lit · lisons · lisez · lisent

**Memory connection / tip:** Reading leads to literature. Compare dire and lire: dis/lis, dit/lit; but dites/lisez.

**Example:** Je lis un livre.  
**English:** I am reading a book.

### 18. écrire — to write

**Present:** écris · écris · écrit · écrivons · écrivez · écrivent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to a written script. The plural stem grows a v: écriv-.

**Example:** J’écris un message.  
**English:** I am writing a message.

### 19. voir — to see

**Present:** vois · vois · voit · voyons · voyez · voient

**Memory connection / tip:** Visual perception: connect to vision. Only nous and vous use voy- in this table.

**Example:** Je vois un ami.  
**English:** I see a friend.

### 20. croire — to believe / to think

**Present:** crois · crois · croit · croyons · croyez · croient

**Memory connection / tip:** Think credo or a belief. Compare voir: vois → crois, voyons → croyons. Croire que introduces what you believe.

**Example:** Je crois que c’est possible.  
**English:** I think it is possible.

### 21. boire — to drink

**Present:** bois · bois · boit · buvons · buvez · boivent

**Memory connection / tip:** Picture three drinking cups: boi- for singular, buv- for nous/vous, boiv- for ils/elles.

**Example:** Je bois de l’eau.  
**English:** I drink water.

### 22. vivre — to live

**Present:** vis · vis · vit · vivons · vivez · vivent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to vivid: full of life. Drop the second v in singular forms; it returns in plural forms.

**Example:** Je vis au Canada.  
**English:** I live in Canada.

### 23. attendre — to wait / to wait for

**Present:** attends · attends · attend · attendons · attendez · attendent

**Memory connection / tip:** Think wait at the bus stop. Attendre takes the thing you await directly: attendre le bus, without pour.

**Example:** J’attends le bus.  
**English:** I am waiting for the bus.

### 24. entendre — to hear

**Present:** entends · entends · entend · entendons · entendez · entendent

**Memory connection / tip:** Your ears hear. Compare attendre and entendre: same endings, different meaning. Hearing is entendre; deliberately listening is écouter.

**Example:** J’entends de la musique.  
**English:** I hear music.

### 25. répondre — to answer / to reply

**Present:** réponds · réponds · répond · répondons · répondez · répondent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to respond. Answer a person or question with répondre à.

**Example:** Je réponds à la question.  
**English:** I answer the question.

### 26. perdre — to lose

**Present:** perds · perds · perd · perdons · perdez · perdent

**Memory connection / tip:** Imagine losing your keys. Same present-tense endings as attendre: -s, -s, nothing, -ons, -ez, -ent.

**Example:** Je perds souvent mes clés.  
**English:** I often lose my keys.

### 27. recevoir — to receive

**Present:** reçois · reçois · reçoit · recevons · recevez · reçoivent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to receive. The ç keeps a soft s sound before o; nous/vous return to recev-.

**Example:** Je reçois un message.  
**English:** I receive a message.

### 28. suivre — to follow

**Present:** suis · suis · suit · suivons · suivez · suivent

**Memory connection / tip:** Think follow a sequence. Je suis can mean I am or I follow; the rest of the sentence gives the meaning.

**Example:** Je suis le guide.  
**English:** I follow the guide. / I am the guide. (Context decides.)

### 29. dormir — to sleep

**Present:** dors · dors · dort · dormons · dormez · dorment

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to dormitory. Drop m from the singular stem; restore it for plural forms.

**Example:** Je dors bien.  
**English:** I sleep well.

### 30. partir — to leave / to depart

**Present:** pars · pars · part · partons · partez · partent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to depart. Drop t from the singular stem; restore it for plural forms.

**Example:** Je pars à huit heures.  
**English:** I leave at eight o’clock.

### 31. sortir — to go out / to exit

**Present:** sors · sors · sort · sortons · sortez · sortent

**Memory connection / tip:** Picture walking out of a door. Like partir, drop t in singular and restore it in plural.

**Example:** Je sors avec mes amis.  
**English:** I go out with my friends.

### 32. sentir — to smell / to feel

**Present:** sens · sens · sent · sentons · sentez · sentent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to senses. Drop t in singular and restore it in plural. For how you feel, learn se sentir: je me sens bien.

**Example:** Je sens le café.  
**English:** I smell the coffee.

### 33. ouvrir — to open

**Present:** ouvre · ouvres · ouvre · ouvrons · ouvrez · ouvrent

**Memory connection / tip:** An exception that behaves like an -er verb in the present: -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent. Think open the door.

**Example:** J’ouvre la porte.  
**English:** I open the door.

### 34. offrir — to offer / to give as a gift

**Present:** offre · offres · offre · offrons · offrez · offrent

**Memory connection / tip:** Connect to offer. It shares ouvrir’s present endings even though both end in -ir.

**Example:** J’offre un cadeau à mon ami.  
**English:** I give my friend a gift.

### 35. courir — to run

**Present:** cours · cours · court · courons · courez · courent

**Memory connection / tip:** Tie it to your own running routine. Keep cour- in every present form; no double r here.

**Example:** Je cours tous les jours.  
**English:** I run every day.

### 36. conduire — to drive / to lead

**Present:** conduis · conduis · conduit · conduisons · conduisez · conduisent

**Memory connection / tip:** Picture conducting a vehicle along a road. Singular condui- becomes conduis- in plural.

**Example:** Je conduis une voiture.  
**English:** I drive a car.

## 3. Five distinctions that prevent common mistakes

| What you mean | French | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I know Marie. | Je connais Marie. | Familiarity with a person. |
| I know where Marie lives. | Je sais où Marie habite. | Knowledge of a fact. |
| I know how to swim. | Je sais nager. | Savoir + infinitive for a learned skill. |
| I am hungry. | J’ai faim. | French uses avoir here. |
| I am tired. | Je suis fatigué / fatiguée. | French uses être here; the adjective agrees. |
| I am waiting for the bus. | J’attends le bus. | No pour after attendre in this sentence. |
| I am answering Paul. | Je réponds à Paul. | Répondre à someone. |
| I feel good. | Je me sens bien. | Se sentir for your own state. |
| I am going to leave. | Je vais partir. | Aller + infinitive: a future plan. |
| I have just left. | Je viens de partir. | Venir de + infinitive: a recent action. |

## 4. Connect the verbs to one day in your life

Read this aloud and picture each action. Bold phrases are ready-to-use chunks.

> **Je suis** au Canada et **j’ai** un objectif : **je veux apprendre** le français. **Je sais** que cela demande de la pratique. Le matin, **je cours**, puis **je bois** de l’eau. **Je mets** ma veste et **je pars** au travail. **J’attends** le bus et **je prends** le bus. Au travail, **je dis** bonjour, **j’entends** des questions et **je réponds** aux clients. Le soir, **je lis** une page et **j’écris** trois phrases. **Je comprends** un peu mieux. Ensuite, **je dors**.

**English:** I am in Canada and I have a goal: I want to learn French. I know that it takes practice. In the morning, I run, then I drink water. I put on my jacket and leave for work. I wait for the bus and take the bus. At work, I say hello, hear questions and answer customers. In the evening, I read a page and write three sentences. I understand a little better. Then I sleep.

## 5. A manageable learning sequence

Treat these as seven sessions, not a deadline. Repeat a session if recall is weak.

| Session | New verbs | Practice focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | être, avoir, aller, faire | Introduce yourself, describe a feeling, name a plan and an activity. |
| 2 | pouvoir, vouloir, devoir, savoir, connaître | Say what you can, want and must do; distinguish the two kinds of knowing. |
| 3 | prendre, apprendre, comprendre, venir, tenir | Reuse the prendre and venir patterns. |
| 4 | dire, lire, écrire, voir, croire | Say what you read, write, see and think. |
| 5 | attendre, entendre, répondre, perdre, mettre, recevoir | Describe waiting, hearing, replying, losing, putting and receiving. |
| 6 | dormir, partir, sortir, sentir, ouvrir, offrir | Contrast shortened singular stems with the ouvrir/offrir endings. |
| 7 | boire, vivre, suivre, courir, conduire | Practice changing stems and describe your routine. |

### Ten-minute practice routine

1. **2 minutes — Recall yesterday’s verbs.** Cover the French and retrieve meaning + je form.
2. **3 minutes — Learn today’s family.** Say je, nous and ils, then all six forms.
3. **3 minutes — Make three personal sentences.** Change the subjects: *je prends → nous prenons → ils prennent*.
4. **2 minutes — Check and repair.** Correct the specific form you missed; say the corrected sentence again.

Review each set the next day, about three days later, and a week later. These intervals are a practical starting point: review sooner when recall is difficult. Reading repeatedly feels easier than recalling; keep the cover-and-recall step.
